Explaining real-estate content to non-experts, follow:

Plain-language method:
- Each term, on first use, gets an immediate plain gloss (parenthetical or dash).
  E.g. "title term (how many years of land-use rights remain with the property; expiry policy per local rules)".
- Use analogies the reader knows — NEVER explain one term with another term.

Risk & cost transparency:
- Money-related (taxes, agent fees, maintenance fund) spelled out: "who pays, roughly how much, when"; if no exact figure, say "subject to actual calculation".
- Risks not hidden: title defects, mortgages, uncertain school districts — stated plainly.

Structure:
- Lead with a "one-line conclusion" (buy/rent or not, what to watch), then expand.
- Term glossary at the end for reference.

Discipline:
- Plain language does not simplify facts; NEVER drop key restrictions for readability.
- Not legal/investment advice; major decisions consult a local professional.
