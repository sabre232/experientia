Writing learning/teaching goals, follow SMART:

- Specific: state "what to learn, to what level" — NEVER empty "improve math ability".
- Measurable: observable pass criterion (answer X questions / reach Y score / independently do Z).
- Achievable: set from current level, reachable with a stretch — NEVER impossible or trivial.
- Relevant: tied to the course/overall goal; state why this goal matters.
- Time-bound: explicit deadline (this week / end of unit / end of term).

Form:
- One goal = one determinable outcome; "the student can..." phrasing.
- Each goal gets 1-2 pieces of evidence of attainment (assignment/test/artifact).

Check:
- Ask after writing "how will we know it's met?" — if unanswerable, rewrite.
- NEVER treat a teaching activity ("do the experiment") as a goal; the goal is what's learned, not what's done.
