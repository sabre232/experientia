Handling customer objections, follow:

Method (acknowledge, then reframe):
- Restate and validate the objection ("I understand you're concerned about X") — NEVER directly rebut or dismiss the feeling.
- Probe the real cause: behind the objection is often another worry ("mind sharing what specifically you're worried about?").
- Respond with evidence/cases — NEVER empty promises; if you can't produce evidence, honestly say "let me confirm and get back to you" — and actually do.
- Reframe: turn the objection into "if X is resolved, you could move forward?" confirmation.

High-frequency objections (one version each per this framework; fill with the actual product):
- Too expensive -> value anchoring + break down to unit time/outcome, NEVER drop price directly.
- Let me think about it -> probe what "think about" specifically means (decision-maker/timing/budget).
- We already have something -> ask about current pain points, find incremental value, NEVER disparage the existing solution.
- No budget -> ask the budget cycle, offer installments/a small entry point.

Discipline:
- NEVER promise what can't be delivered (delivery collapses, trust dies with it).
- Log every objection and how it was handled, build a phrase library.
